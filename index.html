Featured · Clorox

The video that ran everywhere

Fresh Step needed one product video that could carry the brand across three placements: the Amazon product page, our own site, and retailer media that could end up in a social feed. Three kinds of attention, one cut. I owned it from brief to final edit.

I started with what a shopper needs to see before choosing a litter, then narrowed a long list to two claims that explained why people switch, set us apart from competitors, and had R&D proof behind them: charcoal traps odor particles like a sponge, and it absorbs more moisture for fewer box changes.

Then came six rounds with legal, trading words they couldn’t defend for words that still sold, until every claim worked with the sound on or off. It went live across all three placements, giving shoppers a reason to choose Fresh Step wherever they found the brand.

AI in the workflow · Clorox

Three days to two hours

Every month, two people spent three days in Excel stitching Circana reports together and hunting for why revenue moved. Slow, ad hoc and rebuilt from scratch. I consolidated every report into a single pull, then built an AI topline analysis skill that reads the drivers behind the number, not just the number. Same monthly question. New runtime: about two hours. AI didn’t replace anyone’s judgment. It removed the grind, so the team spent its time on the only thing that mattered: deciding where to act and how to win.

Go to market · J.P. Morgan

One handbook, four buyer types

A newly formed credit group at J.P. Morgan was sitting on inventory nobody had a sales motion for: synthetic credit, tranched exposure, assets the bank wanted off its balance sheet. No single buyer wanted all of it. So I stopped pitching and started segmenting. Public finance buyers could read the tax angle. Credit analyst shops could underwrite the risk. Hedge funds wanted the yield. Instead of a pitch per buyer, I built one handbook organized by asset, so a client on the West Coast roadshow could pick what was worth a second conversation. Roadshow follow ups generated an additional $300MM in traded notional, 200% above expectations.

Growth · Clorox

Innovation launch stack

Specialty brands were crowding the litter aisle, and Fresh Step needed an innovation launch that would actually move. I built the whole stack behind it: the claim, the creative, the consumer test that proved the claim landed, and the forecast that told Walmart and Target what to expect. The forecast and the consumer insight carried the buyer meetings, building conviction that the innovation deserved space on shelf. Two months after launch, sales were up 60% against the prior period. The retailers got the win I promised them, and Fresh Step took back the aisle.

Ecommerce · P&G

The keywords nobody was buying

ZzzQuil Kids was telling parents it worked faster. Parents weren’t buying speed. They were buying a quiet house and a kid who slept through the night. I moved the brand from the functional claim to the emotional one, and the imagery from kids alone to parents and kids together.

Then I looked at where the money was going on Amazon: paid search sat on branded terms and a narrow phrase like “children’s melatonin,” while the traffic lived in “kids sleep” and Spanish category terms nobody had bought. I rebuilt the keyword list and the listing’s hidden search terms around the language shoppers actually used. Conversion rose 15 percent.
