Featured · Clorox

The 60 second spot

Cat litter shoppers don’t switch for fun. They switch when the odor wins. Fresh Step needed a 60 second spot with claims strong enough to pull a shopper off the brand already in her cart, and I owned it from the first line of the brief to the final cut. I picked two claims out of a much longer list, each one chosen because it answered why people switch, said something the competition wasn’t saying on shelf, and had an R&D test behind it: charcoal traps odor particles like a sponge, and it absorbs more moisture for fewer box change-outs. Then came the part nobody sees: six rounds with legal, trading words they couldn’t defend for words that still sold. Every round of edits ran through me. It aired. The claims held.

AI in the workflow · Clorox

Three days to two hours

Every month, two people spent three days in Excel stitching Circana reports together and hunting for why revenue moved. Slow, ad hoc, impossible to repeat. I consolidated every report into a single pull, then built an AI topline analysis skill that reads the drivers behind the number, not just the number. Same monthly question. New runtime: about two hours. The AI didn’t replace anyone’s judgment. It removed the grind, so the team spent its time on the only thing that mattered: deciding what to do next.

Go to market · J.P. Morgan

One handbook, four buyers

A newly formed credit group at J.P. Morgan was sitting on inventory nobody had a sales motion for: synthetic credit, tranched exposure, assets the bank wanted off its balance sheet. No single buyer wanted all of it. So I stopped pitching and started segmenting. Public finance buyers could read the tax angle. Credit analyst shops could underwrite the risk. Hedge funds wanted the yield. Instead of a pitch per buyer, I built one handbook organized by asset, so a client on the West Coast roadshow could pick what was worth a second conversation. When swaps stalled on back office booking, I got a PM and an engineering team assigned and drove the client portal build myself. Roadshow follow ups traded an additional $300MM in notional.

Growth · Clorox

The launch stack

Specialty brands were crowding the litter aisle, and Fresh Step needed an innovation launch that would actually move. I built the whole stack behind it: the claim, the creative, the consumer test that proved the claim landed, and the forecast that told Walmart and Target what to expect. Then I walked into those retail meetings and presented it. Two months after launch, sales were up 60 percent against the prior period. Not because any single piece was clever, but because each piece was built to prove the one before it.

Ecommerce · P&G

The keywords nobody was buying

ZzzQuil Kids was telling parents it worked faster. Parents weren’t buying speed. They were buying a quiet house and a kid who slept through the night. I moved the brand from the functional claim to the emotional one, and the imagery from kids alone to parent and child together. Then I looked at where the money was going on Amazon: paid search sat on branded terms and a narrow phrase like “children’s sleep,” while the traffic lived in “kids sleep” and Spanish category terms nobody had bought. I rebuilt the keyword list and the listing’s hidden search terms around the language shoppers actually used. Conversion rose 15 percent.
